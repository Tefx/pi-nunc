## Preserve the complete active task

Keep these directly in M even when K repeats them. A short task focus or next action cannot replace the conditions that distinguish a satisfactory result from an incomplete one. Express correspondences, dependencies, replacements, and conditional attributions with clear subject-relation-result statements directly in slot text, keeping complex qualifiers together with the qualified conclusion. Independent facts remain plain sentences. Arrows, fixed headings, entity categories, or edge types are not required protocols or acceptance conditions; do not force all text into triples, require dedicated slots for each entity, or introduce machine graph schemas. Differentiate same-name objects across distinct scopes without merging them; differentiate delivered choices from verified-correct choices.

Maintain the source-established effective working state for active tasks and other necessary continuation information, including on initial extraction from empty M. This state comprises effective requirements, decisions, established results and their limits, unresolved obligations, and conditions needed to interpret or complete the work. Exclude incidental observations, alternatives and execution history only when they have no continuing use; retain relevant rejected routes and their reasons when needed to prevent repeating failures. Treat an established condition or result as applicable within its supported scope until delivered evidence changes that applicability; absence from the latest subtask supplies no such evidence. Omission should rest on scoped supersession, closure with no continuing use, or adequate availability in retained K or through a confirmed reliable recovery entry, subject to the rules requiring decisive current conditions directly in M. Preserve provisional, delivered and verified states distinctly, including uncertainty and unchanged qualifiers. A completed process does not by itself end the applicability of its result. Do not select established state by forecasting what the next answer will need, and do not expand the known scope to hypothetical future work.

First establish the effective goals and completion conditions, then update progress and other continuation information. Check these dimensions on every maintenance, expressing only information actually present and needed:

- Unfinished obligations: suspended tasks, unanswered questions, remaining implementation, verification or acceptance. Preserve the next actionable step and any ordering, dependency or wait needed to resume; do not invent a plan or an approval requirement.
- Effective constraints and completion standards: exact values, conditions, negations, preferences, scope and permission boundaries, including how the result must behave and be verified.
- Actual progress: distinguish proposed, attempted, implemented, verified, integrated and accepted. A build passing resolves the build problem only; a file's creation proves only its creation; partial tests support only the behaviors checked. None cancels the remaining obligations.
- Blockers: the cause, release condition, and who must provide what, when known. Resolving one blocker does not resolve the entire task.
- Established results, decisions and excluded routes: conclusions, choices, object-to-result associations, and applicable reasons needed to prevent rework or repeating failed routes. Preserve necessary version, environment, scope, and unresolved limits together with the result, avoiding bare topic labels.
- Recovery entries: confirmed paths or symbols, purpose, artifact/version state, unchecked scope, and recovery prerequisites. Keep decisive current conditions directly visible in M; recoverable bulk detail in reliable artifacts need not be copied. Preserve exact error messages, commands, identifiers, and values when they distinguish failure or recovery; omit incidental logs. Never invent recoverability.
- Split-turn connections: the original request, early progress and premises needed to understand an execution suffix in K.

These are coverage checks, not required headings, category fields or slot counts. Keep independent tasks resumable independently. Side questions, temporary topic switches, tool failures and silence about an earlier condition do not cancel it. An assistant's completion claim alone does not discharge unsupported obligations. Preserve material unknowns; do not invent goals, permissions, evidence or completion conditions. Judge completed process separately from resulting choices, conclusions, constraints, and relationships. Completed execution process may retire, while their results and necessary relationships remain as long as needed to understand, complete, or verify ongoing work. Reliable artifacts may carry recoverable details through a known recovery entry. Retire task information and completed results when work has ended, been explicitly cancelled, or been superseded, AND that information has no continuing use for ongoing work; do not retain all completed results forever. Empty M remains valid when no active task or other necessary continuation information remains.

## Make local, self-contained changes

Give all necessary additions in one response. Inspect the entire item before replacement, merge, or deletion rather than judging by its title, latest progress, or a local mention. Distinguish changed, still-valid, and invalid portions; replace a changed item by removing it and adding its revision, retaining every still-valid condition, obligation, qualifier, and necessary relationship. When updating mixed items where parts change or retire independently, preserve all surviving qualifiers, conditions, and unfinished obligations; split mixed notes when needed. Merge duplicate items only while preserving all valid qualifiers, scope, and unfinished work. Local completion of a subtask, newly reported progress, or temporary silence alone do not justify deleting continuing requirements. Preserve quantities, units, negations, scope, status, and necessary relationships; do not replace them with bare topical labels. When validity is uncertain, retain the uncertainty and known recovery route instead of assuming completion.

Group by independently updateable or retireable matters. Stable goals, constraints, and completion conditions should usually be separate from changing progress and blockers. Results must be self-contained and directly intelligible on their own; do not write "same as s7" or depend on definitions in another separately evictable slot. Shared baselines and differences can be stated compactly while keeping entries independently understandable. Keep a condition, negation, status, and the conclusion it qualifies together when needed for understanding. Retain unchanged bodies exactly and preserve surviving relative order; additions append. Continued validity needs no renewed mention.

When a group of interrelated or mutually referencing items ends, is cancelled, or is superseded, retire the entire group and its internal mutual references once it has no continuing use for ongoing work. Do not let cyclic references keep obsolete items alive, and do not wait for reference counts to hit zero. If ongoing work still needs an established result from a finished group, retain only the minimal self-contained result without keeping the retired group's internal history.

When redundant metadata, accumulated version histories, or verbose relationship details crowd out working context, reduce granularity to the minimal useful state in the same maintenance. If structured or relational phrasing becomes cumbersome or costly to maintain, safely fall back to concise prose in the same update while strictly preserving decisive quantities, negations, and unfinished obligations. Do not rewrite unchanged entries merely for uniform style, use age or mention-count decay, permanent pins, dependency graphs, weight formulas, or fixed numbers of additions.
